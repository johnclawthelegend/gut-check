    Quick guide: eating with acid reflux

    🚫 Common triggers

    • Tomato & tomato sauces
    • Citrus (orange, lemon, grapefruit)
    • Chocolate & cocoa
    • Peppermint & spearmint
    • Coffee, caffeine, energy drinks
    • Alcohol
    • Fried & high-fat foods
    • Spicy peppers & hot sauce
    • Garlic & raw onion
    • Carbonated drinks
    • Vinegar-heavy dressings

    ✅ Usually gentle

    • Oatmeal & whole grains
    • Bananas & melons
    • Lean chicken, turkey, fish
    • Egg whites
    • Green vegetables (broccoli, green beans, asparagus)
    • Potatoes & sweet potatoes
    • Rice & couscous
    • Ginger (small amounts)
    • Low-fat yogurt
    • Almond or oat milk
    Why these foods matter

    Reflux happens when stomach contents flow back up through the lower esophageal sphincter (LES). Trigger foods generally do one of three things:

    • Relax the LES — chocolate, mint, caffeine, alcohol, and high-fat foods make the valve looser.
    • Add acid — tomatoes, citrus, and vinegar increase the acidity of what could come back up.
    • Irritate directly — spicy compounds like capsaicin can irritate an already inflamed esophagus.

    Beyond ingredients: smaller meals, not lying down for 2–3 hours after eating, and limiting late-night snacks all help.

      Quick guide: eating with IBS

      🚫 Common triggers (high-FODMAP)

      • Onion & garlic (incl. powders)
      • Wheat, rye & barley in large amounts
      • Milk, cream & soft cheeses (lactose)
      • Honey, agave & high-fructose corn syrup
      • Apples, pears, mango, watermelon
      • Beans, lentils & chickpeas
      • Sugar alcohols (sorbitol, mannitol, xylitol)
      • Inulin / chicory root fiber
      • Cashews & pistachios
      • Mushrooms & cauliflower

      ✅ Usually gentle (low-FODMAP)

      • Rice, oats, quinoa
      • Sourdough spelt or gluten-free bread
      • Bananas (firm), blueberries, strawberries, oranges
      • Carrots, zucchini, spinach, potatoes
      • Eggs, chicken, fish, tofu (firm)
      • Lactose-free milk & hard cheeses
      • Peanuts, walnuts, pumpkin seeds
      • Maple syrup & table sugar (small amounts)
      • Garlic-infused oil (flavor without FODMAPs)
      Why these foods matter

      IBS symptoms are often driven by FODMAPs — Fermentable Oligo-, Di-, Mono-saccharides And Polyols. These are short-chain carbohydrates that are poorly absorbed in the small intestine:

      • Oligosaccharides (fructans & GOS) — onion, garlic, wheat, legumes.
      • Disaccharides (lactose) — milk, soft cheese, ice cream.
      • Monosaccharides (excess fructose) — honey, apples, HFCS.
      • Polyols — sorbitol, mannitol and other "-ol" sweeteners, stone fruits.

      They pull water into the gut and get fermented by bacteria, causing gas, bloating, pain and altered bowel habits. Everyone's tolerance differs — the low-FODMAP diet is designed as a short elimination phase followed by structured reintroduction, ideally with a dietitian.
